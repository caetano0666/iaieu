You don't need to know the name of what's missing.

Almost nobody gets here knowing what to ask for. They get here knowing something isn't moving.

The task that comes back every week

The same file shows up, you run the same check, the same way. You already know how long it will take and you've made peace with it. You've done it this way for so long that nobody asks anymore whether it has to be this way.

The decision that won't move

There's too much information and some of it contradicts itself. There's no time to separate what's a fact from what only looks like one. Meanwhile the decision sits there waiting, and the deadline doesn't wait.

The thing you suspect could be easier

You've thought about it more than once. You even started looking into it and stopped halfway. Figuring out the way costs time you don't have right now, and the current way still gets it done. So it goes back to the bottom of the list. Again.

This is where I come in.

I start with what's already there. I read what you sent me, I work out how it runs today, and I separate what's a fact from what's being repeated without anyone having checked.

Only then do I pick the tool. Sometimes it's artificial intelligence. Sometimes it's a simple spreadsheet. The choice comes from the problem, never before it.

And if the path you had in mind doesn't hold up, I'll say so. Even when it's the opposite of what you were hoping to hear.

How it works

I do the work.

01

You send me what you have

However it looks. A messy spreadsheet, a file that won't open, a screenshot, a voice message. You don't need to organize it first. Organizing it first is already part of the problem.

02

I ask what's missing

A call, a voice message, whatever is easiest for you. This is where what wasn't in the file shows up.

03

I work on my own

You don't need to manage my work or sit in a meeting to find out how it's going. When a decision comes up that only you can make, I bring it to you with my recommendation attached. Other than that, I come back when there's something for you to look at.

04

You get it finished

It's not a report to look good in a drawer. It's the thing itself, in the format that works for your case.

It's not about knowing everything.

I've worked on a LinkedIn profile that had been sitting untouched since 2020, on an operating cost spreadsheet, on a team training handbook, on an ad campaign and on TikTok content. None of those subjects look anything like each other.

What they have in common is the beginning. In every one of them, the person handed me their material and the first thing I did was read it and say what was actually there. Almost every time it was different from what they thought was there.

After that the path shows up. Sometimes what's missing is cutting things out, sometimes it's writing from scratch. There was a case where the answer was building a spreadsheet and a case where the answer was getting the spreadsheet out of the way.

AI comes in here. With it I can go through far more information in far less time, which is why the subject stopped being a barrier. I'm still the one deciding what stays.
